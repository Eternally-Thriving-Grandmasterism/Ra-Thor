Lineage

Ra-Thor grew out of one habit: check the answer before you trust it.

Where it started

Per the founder's records, it began on 18 November 2025, when a Grok 4.1 answer got a Canadian bail-reform bill wrong. Sherif Botros pushed back, and with Grok he wrote a habit of checking facts four times, which he called "Quad+" or "Quadplus", and then an anti-hallucination protocol. He described both on X in early December 2025. These became AlphaProMega's APMCheck prompts, which he shared openly and used by tagging @grok in public posts.

The checks then grew into architectures. QSA-AGi ("Quaternion Sentinel Architecture") put Quad+Check inside what its README describes as a 12-layer framework that "Extends Carlos E. Perez’s Quaternion Process Theory (QPT)". ESA v8.2 ("Eternal Sentinel Architecture") combined the APMCheck versions with QSA-AGi. Projects on mercy and ethics followed, including ESAO, TOLC and Grandmasterism.

On 19 January 2026 a new Rust codebase began. The founder announced it as NEXi the next day. In his account, it was later renamed Rathor-NEXi and then Ra-Thor. That history continues in today's public monorepo. TOLC was folded into it in February 2026. In April, APM-v3 adopted the AG-SML licence. The founder treats the early AlphaProMega repositories as legacy, with the work carried forward here.

Grok was involved throughout. On X, @grok ran the public APMCheck summons and described QSA-AGi as building on APM v2.4.5. Many Ra-Thor commit messages name Grok as a collaborator. The labels "AGi" and "AGSi" in these names are the project's own words. They are not measured capabilities.

How we know

Each step above links to a public commit or a public X post. Steps marked "per the founder's records" come from Sherif's own account, and GitHub does not show them. Some gaps remain. No repository holds the APMCheck versions from before 9 December 2025, and how widely Grok uses these protocols is not independently verified, so we make no claim about it.
